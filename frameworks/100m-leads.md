## The Core Four Advertising Methods
[Source: $100M Leads, p.9, 76]

There are only two types of audiences (warm — people who know you — and cold — strangers) and two ways to reach them (one-to-one/private or one-to-many/public). Crossing those two variables produces the only four things a single person can ever do to let someone know about their stuff: Warm Outreach, Posting Content, Cold Outreach, and Paid Ads. Hormozi treats this as the entire universe of advertising — everything else is a variant, combination, or scaled version of these four.

> "Advertising, the process of making known, lets strangers know about the stuff you sell. If more people know about the stuff you sell, then you sell more stuff." (p.9)
> "Combining warm and cold audiences with 1-to-1 and 1-to-many leads us to the only four ways we can let anyone know about anything: the core four." (p.76)

**Apply:** Stop hunting for a secret advertising channel — pick one of the four, master it with volume, then layer the next.

## Lead Magnets — Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation
[Source: $100M Leads, p.48, 65]

Leads alone aren't enough; you need engaged leads (people who show interest). A lead magnet is a complete, valuable solution to a narrow problem, given away free or cheap, that reveals a bigger problem only your core offer can solve — like salty bar pretzels that make people thirsty enough to buy a drink. The lead magnet should be so good it could be sold on its own; the fear of "giving away too much" is backwards, since a mediocre free offer trashes your reputation before you ever get a chance to sell.

> "A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem... once solved, it reveals another problem solved by your core offer." (p.48)
> "The marketplace judges everything you have to offer - free or not. And you can never provide too much value. But, you can provide too little." (p.65)

**Apply:** Before advertising your core offer, build one free/cheap lead magnet that solves a single narrow problem so well people feel obligated to buy the next step.

## Warm Outreach — The First Five Clients System
[Source: $100M Leads, p.82, 104]

Warm outreach is one-to-one contact with people who already know you (contacts, followers, past customers) — the cheapest, most reliable way to get first customers, and the foundation every other method sits on. The 10-step system: pull every list you have, reach out to 100 people/day with a personalized message, use the Acknowledge-Compliment-Ask (ACA) framework to build rapport, make your first five clients an easy free offer in exchange for testimonials and referrals, then raise price by 20% every five sign-ups.

> "Warm reach outs are when you make one-to-one contact with your warm audience... It's the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff you sell." (p.82)
> "Warm reach outs should get about one in five contacts to engage." (p.104)

**Apply:** Before spending a dollar on ads, message 100 people you already know per day about your offer or lead magnet.

## Cold Outreach — Personalize, Then Give Big Fast Value
[Source: $100M Leads, p.164, 170]

Cold outreach is one-to-one contact with strangers — a pure numbers game solved in three steps: build a targeted list (scrape, buy from brokers, or hand-collect), personalize the opener so it reads like a warm reach-out, then overwhelm them with disproportionately generous free value in the first message since strangers give you seconds, not minutes, to earn trust. It's slower to build than paid ads but nearly impossible for competitors to copy or shut down, since it's entirely private.

> "Cold outreach is a numbers game. The more people you reach out to the more engaged leads you get." (p.164)
> "If you forget everything about this chapter, remember one thing: the goal is to demonstrate big value as fast as possible." (p.170)

**Apply:** For every cold message, spend more effort on one line of real personalization and one absurdly generous free offer than on the pitch itself.

## Posting Free Content — The Content Unit & Give-Give-Give-Ask
[Source: $100M Leads, p.114-115, 132]

Every piece of audience-growing content, short or long, is built from the same three-part "content unit": Hook (earn attention with topic, headline, format), Retain (keep them curious with lists, steps, stories), Reward (actually satisfy what the hook promised). To monetize the resulting audience without killing its growth, dramatically over-give relative to how often you ask — Hormozi's rule of thumb is "give until they ask," letting the audience volunteer to buy rather than being pitched constantly.

> "A content unit has three components - Hook, retain, and reward." (p.114-115)
> "Give until they ask... The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth." (p.132-133)

**Apply:** Post one complete hook-retain-reward content unit per day on one platform before you ever insert a sales ask.

## Paid Ads — Call Out, Value, CTA, and Client Financed Acquisition
[Source: $100M Leads, p.196, 220, 222]

A profitable ad has three parts: a Call Out that hooks the exact right stranger (label, yes-question, if-then), Value that proves the offer using the value equation (dream outcome, likelihood, speed, ease) across What-Who-When angles, and a clear CTA. Efficiency, not creativity, wins — track your LTGP:CAC ratio (aim above 3:1) and use "client financed acquisition": structure pricing and upsells so a customer pays back their full acquisition cost within 30 days, turning ad spend into a self-funding, infinitely scalable machine.

> "Call Out + Value + Call to Action (CTA)" (p.196)
> "As soon as I get it above 3 to 1 (either through decreasing CAC or increasing LTGP), they take off." (p.220)
> "Figure out a way to get your customers to pay you back in the first thirty days so you can recycle your cash to get more customers." (p.222)

**Apply:** Before scaling ad spend, confirm your customer pays back their full acquisition cost within 30 days — if not, fix the offer/upsell before adding more budget.

## Core Four On Steroids — More, Better, New
[Source: $100M Leads, p.233-234, 242]

When engaged leads plateau, the fix is never "the market is saturated" (the Size of the Pie Fallacy) — it's one of three levers: do MORE of what already works (the Rule of 100: 100 primary actions/day for 100 days), do it BETTER by testing one variable per week at your biggest constraint (the step with the largest drop-off), or go NEW — new placements, new platforms, then an entirely new Core Four activity, in that order.

> "1) You can do more of what you're currently doing. 2) You can do what you're currently doing better. 3) You can do it somewhere new." (p.233)
> "The rule of 100 is simple. You advertise your stuff by doing 100 primary actions every day, for one hundred days in a row." (p.234)

**Apply:** When leads stall, ask "what's stopping me from doing 10x what I'm currently doing?" before assuming the market is tapped.

## Customer Referrals — Word of Mouth Is Exponential
[Source: $100M Leads, p.262, 265]

Referrals are the cheapest, highest-quality leads because they compound: the Core Four scale linearly with effort, but word of mouth is exponential — one customer brings two, two bring four. Most businesses get few referrals for two fixable reasons: the product isn't remarkable enough (goodwill = value minus price), and nobody actually asks. Build goodwill via six levers (better-fit customers, lower promises/bigger delivery, better results, faster wins, less effort required, always having a next thing to sell), then explicitly ask using a one- or two-sided incentive.

> "Price is what you charge. Value is what they get. The difference between price and value is goodwill." (p.265)
> "With word of mouth, one customer brings two. Two bring four. Four bring eight. And so forth. It's not linear, it's exponential." (p.262)

**Apply:** Pick one of the six goodwill levers to improve this month, then explicitly ask every new customer for a named-friend introduction at the moment they buy.

## Lead Getters — Leverage Through Employees, Agencies, and Affiliates
[Source: $100M Leads, p.248-249, 319]

Beyond the Core Four you can do yourself, four types of "lead getters" advertise on your behalf: customers (referrals), employees (trained to run the same Core Four internally), agencies (rented short-term to teach you a new platform, not to run it forever), and affiliates (independent businesses that sell your stuff to their own warm audience for a cut). Leverage is defined as output per hour of your own work — the affiliate army is the highest-leverage version, since each new affiliate becomes a permanent, compounding source of leads with zero ongoing advertising spend from you.

> "That means leverage boils down to how much we get for the time we spend getting it... I call these other people lead getters." (p.248-249)
> "The best part is, I didn't advertise or sell any of the products at all. No paid ads. No sales team. Nothing. The affiliates did everything." (p.319)

**Apply:** For your next hire or partnership, ask "does this let someone else do the Core Four for me" before asking "does this let me do more of it myself."
